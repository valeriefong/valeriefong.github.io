Research experience

2022 – present
Graduate Student Researcher, University of California, Davis
  • Developed a Python/PyTorch simulation pipeline with SLURM job arrays to study Lyapunov spectra in three recurrent network models, scaling to N = 2,000 neurons across 1,060 parameter combinations: about 25,000 simulation runs and 70,000 CPU core-hours on an HPC cluster.
  • Adapted and extended a published Lyapunov-spectrum solver using the QR/Benettin method, fixed-step RK4 integration, and tangent dynamics; validated the pipeline against a reference implementation and assessed numerical convergence through paired comparisons of step size, orthonormalization interval, simulation duration, precision, and network size.
  • Analyzing the resulting simulation dataset to determine how two stabilizing mechanisms shape the dependence of Lyapunov spectra on network size and gain, relative to a classical random-network baseline.
  • Explored Dynamic Mode Decomposition on high-dimensional neural recordings, working with the experimental neuroscientists who collected the data to understand its biological and experimental context.
2021 – 2022
Ronald E. McNair Scholar and ASSURE Fellow, University of New Mexico
  • Applied variance-based Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis to quantify how uncertainty in eight model parameters influences Hodgkin–Huxley single-neuron model outputs across hundreds of thousands of simulations.
  • Presented research findings at three national McNair research conferences.
