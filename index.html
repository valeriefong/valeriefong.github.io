Valerie Fong

I’m an applied mathematician finishing a PhD at UC Davis. I study recurrent networks with many oscillatory modes held at the edge of stability, and how their activity can generate long, non-repeating sequences that other neurons read out as time.

Three ideas behind my work

Modes near the edge of stability

A linear network’s activity is a sum of modes, each an oscillation that grows or decays at a rate set by its eigenvalue. A mode whose eigenvalue sits just left of the imaginary axis fades very slowly, so it can carry information for a long time.

How I use many of them at once

Quasiperiodicity

Two oscillations together move a point around a torus. If their frequencies have a rational ratio, the path closes and repeats. If the ratio is irrational, it never does. A network with many such frequencies traces a path that takes an extremely long time to come back.

Measuring how long it takes to return

The transition to chaos

In the classic random network of Sompolinsky, Crisanti and Sommers (1988), raising the gain g pushes the connectivity’s eigenvalues past a boundary, and quiet activity gives way to self-sustained, chaotic activity. Only a few eigenvalues cross at first. My work asks what happens when many cross together.
