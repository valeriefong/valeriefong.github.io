Valerie Fong

I’m an applied mathematician finishing a PhD at UC Davis (expected December 2027). My research involves building and simulating large dynamical models of recurrent neural networks. My goal is to understand how many interacting units jointly give rise to behaviors that may support information processing and computation in the brain.

In my PhD research I own a project end to end, from experimental design to analysis. I built a Python/PyTorch simulation pipeline with SLURM job arrays covering 1,060 parameter combinations for three recurrent network models (up to 2,000 units), generating about 22,000 runs over nearly 90,000 CPU core-hours. I adapted and extended a published Lyapunov-spectrum solver (QR/Benettin method, RK4 integration), validated it against a reference implementation, and checked numerical convergence via paired comparisons of step size, reorthonormalization interval, duration, and precision. I’m now analyzing the resulting dataset to compare how two stabilizing mechanisms shape sensitivity to perturbation (full Lyapunov spectra, attractor dimension, entropy) across network size and gain, against a baseline random network. A manuscript is in preparation.

Before graduate school I was a data science intern at iRhythm Technologies, where I explored time-series forecasting approaches on multi-year daily product-order data, and an intern at Sandia National Laboratories, where I compiled incident data, maintained a metrics dashboard, and earned a Lean Six Sigma Green Belt.

Current project: chaos in recurrent networks

When the coupling between units is weak, activity dies away. When it is strong enough, the network keeps itself active with no input, and two almost identical starting states drift apart: the network is chaotic. The Lyapunov spectrum measures that sensitivity, one direction at a time.
